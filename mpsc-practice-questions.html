Arithmetic — 19 topics

Three per topic, easy → exam-level. Work left to right; the reveal shows the full method, not just the number.

A1

Numbers · Surds · HCF/LCM

1. Find the HCF of 36 and 60.

Show answer
36=2²·3², 60=2²·3·5 → common = 2²·3 = 12.

2. Simplify √50 + √18.

Show answer
√50=5√2, √18=3√2 → 5√2+3√2 = 8√2.

3. Find the LCM of 12, 15 and 20.

Show answer
Take highest powers: 2²·3·5 = 60.
A2

AP / Sequences

1. Find the 15th term of the AP 3, 7, 11, …

Show answer
aₙ = a+(n−1)d = 3+14·4 = 59.

2. Sum of the first 20 terms of 2, 4, 6, …

Show answer
Sₙ = n/2·[2a+(n−1)d] = 10·[4+38] = 420.

3. Which term of 5, 8, 11, … equals 50?

Show answer
5+(n−1)3 = 50 → n−1 = 15 → n = 16th term.

Hard-part drill — the traps

These mirror the tricky questions from the papers. Same five moves apply: restate → spot the trap → set up → test → generalise. Don't peek until you've committed to an answer.

H1

Same selling price, opposite percentages

1. Two watches sold at the same price; one at +20%, one at −20%. Overall?

Show answer
Equal SP with ±x% → x²/100% loss = 400/100 = 4% loss (never zero).Trap: the percentages sit on different cost prices, so they can't cancel.

2. Same idea with ±10%. Overall result?

Show answer
10²/100 = 1% loss.

3. A discount of 20% then a further 25% on marked ₹1000. Final price?

Show answer
1000·0.8·0.75 = ₹600 (not a flat 45% off).Successive discounts multiply; they don't add to 45%.
H2

Two ratios bridged by a shared fact

1. Incomes 4:3, expenditures 3:2, each saves ₹12,000. Total income?

Show answer
4k−3m=12000, 3k−2m=12000 → k=m → k=12000 → total 7k = ₹84,000.Give each ratio its own multiplier; turn each sentence into an equation.

2. Two numbers in ratio 5:3; their difference is 10. Larger number?

Show answer
5k−3k = 2k = 10 → k=5 → larger = 25.

3. A:B = 2:3 and B:C = 4:5. If C = 30, find A.

Show answer
A:B:C = 8:12:15. C=15 units=30 → 1 unit=2 → A=8·2 = 16.
H3

Equal area / equal quantity → one equation

1. A square and rectangle have equal area; rectangle is (side+8) by (side−6). Perimeter of rectangle?

Show answer
s² = (s+8)(s−6) → s=24 → 32×18 → perimeter = 100 cm.Set the two areas equal; the s² terms cancel and leave a linear solve.

2. A leap year is 366 days. How many odd days does it add?

Show answer
366 mod 7 = 2.

3. A works in 36 days, B in 48 days. A works x days, B works (x+2) days, doing ⅓ of the job. Find x.

Show answer
x/36 + (x+2)/48 = 1/3 → ×144 → 7x+6=48 → x = 6.Convert everyone to work-per-day before adding — days never add directly.
