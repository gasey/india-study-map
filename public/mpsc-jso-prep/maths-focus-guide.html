01 How to use this, and why Maths first

Why Mathematics before the rest of Paper III

Paper III is 200 marks: General Science 100 (Physics 34, Chemistry 36, Biology or Mathematics 30) plus General Forensic 100. Physics and Chemistry are vast — 70 marks across mechanics, thermodynamics, electrodynamics, atomic physics, organic chemistry and electrochemistry. Mathematics is 30 marks across three compact topics with a fixed formula set and no breadth to hide in. It is the fastest block to take from "unprepared" to "full marks", and the negative-marking arithmetic rewards exactly the precision this unit tests.

Read this before you trust an answer

No JSO (Cyber Forensic) paper has ever been set — the division has never been recruited — so there is no official answer key for this unit. Questions marked real MPSC are transcribed from actual MPSC papers at a matching standard (Inspector of Statistics, Sub Inspector (UB), High School Teacher Mathematics); their answers are derived from subject knowledge and cross-checked. All other questions are authored to the official syllabus. Every answer below was verified computationally before publication.

The order that works

Read the unit table to see the three blocks. For each block, read its primer, write the formulae from memory, then drill that block's solved questions. Finish with the 30-mark mock under negative marking.

What actually earns marks

Every question carries a trap (the distractor that catches people and why) and variations (other ways the same concept gets asked). Those generalise; the exact question will almost certainly not repeat verbatim.

A repeatable study loop

1 · Diagnose: pick one sub-topic from the unit table. 2 · Learn: read its primer and write the formulae or definitions from memory. 3 · Retrieve: answer that topic's questions without looking at the explanation. 4 · Repair: record the trap you fell for, then revisit after 1 day, 3 days and 7 days. 5 · Test: use the timed mock only after topic-level recall is working.

02 The 30 marks at a glance

The official syllabus splits Unit III into three equal 10-mark blocks. The bars show how many solved questions this guide provides per block — the coverage is even, because the mark split is even.

Official syllabus text (verbatim)

I. Sets, Relations and functions (10 Marks): Sets and their representation; Union, intersection and complement of sets and their algebraic properties; Power set, Relations, type of relations, equivalence relations, functions; one-one, into and onto functions, the composition of functions.

II. Permutations and combinations (10 Marks): The fundamental principle of counting, permutations and combinations; Meaning of P(n, r) and C(n, r). Simple applications.

III. Statistics and probability (10 Marks): Measures of dispersion; calculation of mean, median, mode of grouped and ungrouped data, calculation of standard deviation, variance and mean deviation for grouped and ungrouped data. Probability: Probability of an event, addition and multiplication theorems of probability.

06 Method, provenance and honest limits

Where the questions come from

Three real MPSC papers supplied questions that sit exactly on the JSO syllabus: Inspector of Statistics Paper-III (Mathematics), 2017 (the surjection question), Sub Inspector (UB) Mathematics, 2018 (the prime-number and red-Queen probability questions), and High School Teacher (Mathematics) Paper-I, 2017 (the standard-deviation transformation and exactly-one-girl questions). The remaining questions are authored to the official syllabus text and verified computationally.

What is not covered here

The JSO paper has never been set, so no question can be guaranteed to predict it. The nearest real MPSC mathematics papers (ACF, MCS Main, MPE&SS) are set at a far higher level — matrices, real analysis, abstract algebra — and are not useful for this unit. The syllabus here is deliberately elementary; the value is in total command of a small surface, not in advanced material.

Verification

Every numerical answer in this guide was recomputed by script before publication, and the real-paper questions were checked against their source OCR. Where a real paper's OCR was ambiguous the question was dropped rather than guessed. If you find a discrepancy, treat the arithmetic as authoritative and check the source paper.
