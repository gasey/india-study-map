01 How to use this, and what it is not

Read this before you trust a single answer

No official answer key exists for any of these papers. MPSC has published keys for 120 exams, none of them for the Physics, Chemistry, JSO-Chemistry or General-Science papers used here. Every answer below was therefore derived from subject knowledge and then reviewed, not transcribed from a key. Each question carries a confidence badge; 119 of 134 are marked high confidence. Where an answer is genuinely arguable it says so. Treat any disagreement with your textbook as the textbook winning.

Also: these papers are not your paper

The JSO (Cyber Forensic) division has never been recruited, so there is no past JSO Cyber Forensic paper in existence. What you get instead is the nearest real evidence of how MPSC writes science MCQs: the 2016 JSO (Chemistry) papers from the same Home (Forensic) department, the Legal Metrology Physics and Chemistry papers, and the Forest Ranger General Science papers. Difficulty is anchored to that JSO 2016 level.

The order that works

Start at the high-yield ranking to see where the marks actually sit — it is not evenly spread. Read the primer for a topic, then drill that topic's real past questions. Finish with a timed mock under negative marking.

The two fields worth most

Every solved question carries a trap (the distractor that catches people and why) and variations (other ways the same concept gets asked). Those generalise; the exact question almost certainly will not repeat verbatim.

A repeatable study loop

1 · Diagnose: use the ranking and gaps table to choose one sub-topic. 2 · Learn: read its primer and write the formulae or definitions from memory. 3 · Retrieve: answer the matching past questions without looking at the explanation. 4 · Repair: record the trap you fell for, then revisit the topic after 1 day, 3 days and 7 days. 5 · Test: use a timed mixed mock only after topic-level recall is working. This turns the guide into a loop, not a chapter to read once.

Weak topic

Primer → closed-book formula dump → 5–10 questions → error note.

Almost ready

Mix two nearby topics and explain why each distractor is wrong.

Exam mode

Timed mock → review every miss → revise only the weak-topic list.

08 Resource strategy — which model for which stage

This guide was produced by a pipeline whose expensive steps were deliberately kept small. If you rebuild or extend it, spend tokens in this order of preference — cheapest first.

1 · No model at all

Parsing OCR text, segmenting MCQs, reconstructing question numbers from position, detecting two-column bleed, topic classification by regex, frequency counts, near-duplicate detection, batching, HTML assembly. All of it is deterministic Python. Roughly 85% of the work here cost zero tokens. A language model that is asked to "read these files and count topics" burns a fortune doing badly what twenty lines of Python do exactly.

2 · Cheap model (Haiku-class)

Yes/no triage, dropping obvious non-science items, tidying option text, flagging garbled stems for review, deduplicating near-identical wording. Anything where the answer is short and a mistake is cheap and visible.

3 · Mid model (Sonnet-class) — the workhorse

Solving the MCQs and writing the explanations, analogies, traps and variations. This is 11th-standard-to-BSc science; a mid model handles it reliably. All 134 solved questions and all 45 primers here were produced this way, in six and three parallel batches respectively. Requiring a confidence field on every answer is what makes this safe — it converts "trust the model" into "trust the model, verify the 10% it flagged".

4 · Top model (Opus-class) — only where judgement is the product

Deciding scope against the official syllabus, spotting that source papers are mixed-subject, catching the classifier bug where cM matched "cm" in wavenumbers and filed spectroscopy under genetics, adjudicating the flagged low-confidence items, and ruling three questions unscoreable. Reviewing 15 flagged items costs a fraction of solving 134 — that asymmetry is the whole trick.

The pattern, in one line

Deterministic code for anything mechanical → mid model in parallel batches for bulk authoring, each item self-reporting confidence → top model only on what got flagged, plus the design decisions. Escalate on evidence, never by default.

09 Method, provenance and honest limits

How the questions were recovered

Each source PDF has a sibling .ocr.txt. Printed question numbers in those files are unreliable — OCR strips them to - or mangles 82 into 32 — so questions were segmented by their option blocks and numbered by position instead. Option markers appear as (a), {a), ©), () and more, all normalised. Anything with more than four markers is two-column bleed and was discarded rather than guessed.

What is still damaged

Subscripts and superscripts are routinely destroyed: O, for O2, CIF, for ClF3, sp' for sp3, and λ read as A. Where the intended meaning was certain it was reconstructed and the reconstruction is stated on the question. Where it was not, the question was dropped — four were, plus two more ruled unscoreable.

Subject labels are content-derived

The source papers are mixed-subject: the "Chemistry" papers carry biology, English vocabulary and even an NDPS Act question; the Forest Ranger "General Science" papers carry physics, geology and statistics. Filenames were therefore used only as provenance, and every question was re-classified from its own text.

The biology problem

Only 71 of the 777 questions are biology, and the JSO biology syllabus asks for cell biology, physiology and genetics while these papers lean to botanical systematics. Membrane transport has zero past questions. Biology is the component where this guide is thinnest, and the gaps table says exactly where.
